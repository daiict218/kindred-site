LDL cholesterol test: normal range, high and low meaning

LDL is called 'bad' cholesterol because it builds up in the walls of blood vessels.

Usual range

  • Optimal: below 100 mg/dL
  • Near optimal: 100 to 129 mg/dL
  • Borderline high: 130 to 159 mg/dL
  • High: 160 mg/dL or more
  • With diabetes or heart disease, doctors often aim much lower (below 70, or below 55)

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is high

High LDL raises the risk of heart attack and stroke over time. Less fried and packaged food, more fibre, exercise and not smoking help; many people also need a medicine such as a statin.

If it is low

Low LDL is usually good.

When to see a doctor soon

Talk to a doctor if LDL is 160 mg/dL or more, or above your target if you have diabetes or heart disease.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What LDL target is right for me?
  • Do I need a statin?
  • How soon should I recheck?
