CRP (C-reactive protein) test: normal range, high and low meaning

CRP is made by the liver when there is inflammation or infection in the body. It rises within hours.

Usual range

  • Usually below about 5 mg/L (labs differ: some use 6 or 10)
  • hs-CRP, a more sensitive version, is used for heart risk: below 1 mg/L is low risk, above 3 mg/L is higher risk

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is high

A high CRP means inflammation somewhere: an infection, an injury, arthritis or another inflammatory disease. Very high values (above about 100 mg/L) often mean a bacterial infection. CRP does not show where the problem is.

If it is low

A low CRP is normal.

When to see a doctor soon

See a doctor soon if CRP is high with a fever, or above about 50 mg/L.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What could be causing the inflammation?
  • Do I need antibiotics or other tests?
  • When should CRP be repeated?

Also printed on reports as

  • CRP
  • C-Reactive Protein
