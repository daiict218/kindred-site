TSH (thyroid test) test: normal range, high and low meaning

TSH (thyroid stimulating hormone) is made by a gland in the brain. It tells the thyroid how much hormone to make, so it is the main test of thyroid function.

Usual range

  • Adults: about 0.4 to 4.0 µIU/mL (labs differ a little)
  • In pregnancy, the targets are lower and your doctor sets them

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is high

A high TSH usually means an underactive thyroid (hypothyroidism): tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold, constipation, dry skin, heavy periods. It is common in Indian women and is treated with a daily tablet.

If it is low

A low TSH usually means an overactive thyroid (hyperthyroidism): weight loss, fast heartbeat, sweating, shaking, feeling anxious. It can also come from too high a dose of thyroid tablets.

When to see a doctor soon

See a doctor if TSH is outside the range, and soon if you are pregnant or planning a pregnancy.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Do I need T3 and T4 tests too?
  • Do I need a tablet, or should we recheck first?
  • If I take a thyroid tablet, is my dose right?
