Bilirubin test: normal range, high and low meaning

Bilirubin is a yellow substance made when old red blood cells break down. The liver clears it into the bile.

Usual range

  • Total bilirubin: about 0.3 to 1.2 mg/dL
  • Direct bilirubin: up to about 0.3 mg/dL

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is high

High bilirubin causes jaundice (yellow eyes and skin). It can come from hepatitis, a blocked bile duct (for example from gallstones) or fast breakdown of red cells. A small rise in indirect bilirubin with normal liver enzymes is often Gilbert's syndrome, a harmless condition that runs in families.

If it is low

A low bilirubin is not a concern.

When to see a doctor soon

See a doctor soon if there are yellow eyes or skin, dark urine, pale stools or belly pain.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is it direct or indirect bilirubin that is high?
  • Are my liver enzymes normal?
  • Do I need an ultrasound?

Also printed on reports as

  • Bilirubin, total
  • Total Bilirubin
  • Serum Bilirubin Total
