Post-meal blood sugar (PPBS) test: normal range, high and low meaning

Post-meal (post-prandial) blood sugar is the glucose in your blood 2 hours after you start a meal. It shows how well your body handles the sugar from food.

Usual range

  • Normal: below 140 mg/dL
  • Prediabetes: 140 to 199 mg/dL
  • Diabetes: 200 mg/dL or more

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is high

High post-meal sugar is often the first sign of diabetes. It can be high even when fasting sugar is normal.

If it is low

Below 70 mg/dL after a meal is unusual. Tell your doctor, especially if you take diabetes medicine.

When to see a doctor soon

See a doctor if post-meal sugar is often 200 mg/dL or more. If it is below 70 mg/dL with shaking or sweating, take sugar or juice now.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Is my post-meal sugar in the diabetes range?
  • Should I check HbA1c?
  • How should I change my meals?
