Vitamin B12 test: normal range, high and low meaning

Vitamin B12 is needed for healthy nerves and to make red blood cells. It comes mainly from milk, curd, paneer, eggs, fish and meat.

Usual range

  • Usual: about 200 to 900 pg/mL
  • Low: below 200 pg/mL; 200 to 300 is borderline

Ranges differ between labs, and by age and sex. Use the range printed on your report.

If it is low

Low B12 is common in vegetarians and in people who take metformin or acidity medicines for a long time. It can cause tiredness, anaemia, tingling or numbness in the hands and feet, and poor memory.

If it is high

High B12 usually comes from supplements or injections and is rarely a concern by itself.

When to see a doctor soon

See a doctor if B12 is low and you have tingling, numbness, poor balance or memory problems.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • Do I need tablets or injections?
  • Should I check my haemoglobin and folate?
  • Could a medicine I take be the cause?
